# Caprican Lore Context

The human history of Columbia began with the migration of seafaring hunter-gatherers. Since, the majority of Columbian history was isolated from Albuemani contact until the age of discovery.
Prehistoric Caprica
The ancestors of Columbia's indigenous people are the Paleo-Columbians, hunter-gatherers who excelled in seafaring. The Paleo-Columbians crossed vast distances from Albuemanto settle the new world, but were trapped following the start of the first ice age. Due to the hardships of the ice age, the Paleo-Columbians abandoned their seafaring traditions and formed various new cultures. After the age of discovery, beginning in 829, Columbia was colonized by several colonial empires, including the Gallian Empire, Angdorian Kingdom (later Alanian Empire), and Venetarran Empire. Gallia colonized much of the western half of the continent, including Caprica, Trinconpore, and Maraiseille. In the majority of cases, with the exception of the Gallian-Indigenous War, Gallian control over western Columbia ran uncontested. In the east, Gallian rivals Alania and Venetarra formed their own colonial territories, with Angdoria taking the northern continent and Venetarra colonizing the eastern half of Marais. 
As part of efforts to settle the Gallian colonies, Gallia relied on the mass-importation of Kemeti slaves.[1] This resulted in a long term significant population of people of Kemeti ancestry in Caprica, who would face centuries of segregation and economic disparity even after Caprican liberalization.
The Glorious Revolution
The first wave of independence took place during Angdoria's transition to the Alanian Empire, following a liberal revolt during the Glorious Revolution of 902. In exchange for colonial support during the revolution, the new United Islands of Alania granted independence to its northern colonies, raising tensions amongst colonies under Gallian and Venetarran control.
War of Gallian Succession
In Gallia, the death of the last ruler of the main royal family led to a war between the Siloriel family and the Montoibleuax family. The first phase of the war, beginning in 1669, lasted until 1671 and saw the devastation of the Gallian economy by various interceding empires attempting to interfere in the war effort. The Alanian Empire, in support of the Siloriel family, maintained a yearslong blockade of Gallia, while the Montoibleaux family was supported by an Uralvoskian invasion of Siloriel territories. This invasion proved decisive, and the Siloriel family vacated the Gallian mainland and were ferried to Trinconpore by the Alanian navy.
From 1671 to 1673, Uralvosk and Gallia staged three attempts to invade Trinconpore through the Gallian colony of Maraiseille. The first two attempts were deterred by the Alanian navy, but the third attempt saw a large landing force gather in the northern duchy of Lorient. In an effort to repel the invading army, the ruling monarch signed an edict liberating all Orenseans on the isle of Trinconpore from Gallian corvée labor. This unprecedented edict, which removed all legal segregation between Albuemanis and Orenseans in one stroke, resulted in a surge of Orensean free-men joining the badly-attrited Siloriel forces. In what was known as the Battle of Lorient, Albuemani and Orensean forces loyal to the Siloriel family, supplied and supported by the Alanian navy, inflicted a devastating defeat to the Gallian and Uralvoskian forces on Trinconpore.
The end of the War of Gallian Succession, had major ramifications for the world, along with Caprica. Alania, now an uncontested world power, held firm control over the waterways of Perdita. Uncontested on the world stage, the world experienced a period of Alanianization, nowhere felt better than the gradual replacement of Gallian language with Alanian in Caprica. Additionally, the independence of Trinconpore ended Gallian ease of access to the Aortic Sea, placing massive pressure on Gallia's colonies to secure their own future. 
Simultaneously, the Montoibleaux family sought to establish a ‘revival’ of monarchal power, with the now famous King Louie d’Montoibleaux forming the basis of what modern historians call absolutism. Concentrating wealth into and shifting political influence from the nobility, the Montoibleaux reigned with an unprecedented mandate. Even still, difficulties accessing its colonies and a foreign policy which saw Gallia in decline created a powder-keg of public sentiment.
Just 80 years after the loss of Trinconpore and the accession of the Montoibleaux, internal conflicts within Gallia would lead to the Gallian Revolutions, also known as the Revolutions of 1754, during which a wave of republican fervor rocked Albueman. Across the Gallian Empire, various revolters seized power locally, leading to a widespread movement of emancipation which the Gallian mainland was unable to control. 
The loss of Trinconpore, and the brief, hundred-day ‘Pleucadeucian Republic’, made the situation untenable–the monarchy was deposed, and after successive periods of anarchy, the Gallian government was seized by the military general Pleucadeuc,who went on to conquer much of continental Albueman. Fleeing the rule of the Pleucadeucians, the Montoibleaux family fled to the loyalist stronghold of Caprica and put down the revolters of the Pleucadeucian Republic, establishing the Most Serene Empire of Caprica.
Montoibleaux Rule
1754 is considered by many to be the year of Caprican independence, but Caprica remained shackled by the Gallian monarchy. Despite initial Caprican dominance in the Aortic by share of trade, Montoibleaux rule stagnated Caprican development, which was rapidly outpaced by newly-liberated nations such as Maraiseille. The dynamism of Maraiseille’s liberal movement, combined with a lack of allies due to isolationist policies, prevented Montoibleauxan Caprica from expanding its influence. Neighboring Trinconpore liberalized in 1813, and following the Gallian Revolutions, shifting balances of power in Albueman saw Veneterra relinquished control of its colonies, resulting in the near-complete 'end' to colonial rule. Whereas the Montoibleaux ruled Caprica alone, they continued to rule Caprica as a colony.
In 1897, the Montoibleaux dynasty began the process of liberalizing Caprica and ending the longstanding policies of isolationism. Negotiations between the monarchy and would-be industrialists, academics, and members of the nobility progressed relatively quickly, with elections called that saw the first Prime Minister of Caprica, Scarlett Barnes, sworn in. She was the daughter of Lord Kaden Powell, a staunch traditionalist magnate chief amongst the old nobility of the imperial period. As a result, many republicans in Caprica consider 1897 to be the true year of Caprican independence, with the establishment of a modern, legislative state. The Barnes premiership set about a process of state-building, and establishing a rule of law within Caprica’s territory in the absence of Montoibleaux rule.
The second Prime Minister of Caprica, Alan Bluespan, began the process of liberalizing Caprica’s previously mercantilist economy. His liberalization attempts were opposed by the head of the conservative faction, Kaden, who succeeded in toppling Alan’s government, but failure to establish a new government led to Alan’s renomination to the premiership.
Rochelist Insurrection of 1899
Following the legislative chaos of Alan’s ousting, a rochelist faction of the United People’s Party attempted to stage a revolt. Attempting to seize parliament, coordinated attacks occurred in Kazana, Mezata, and Costa Blanca, before their defeat at the hands of the royal guard. Rochelist parties would be banned following the United People’s Party, with Alan as the sole dissenting vote.
In 1901, Scarlett assumed the premiership, leading a minority government following a a failure by the left-wing to nominate a Prime Minister. This was due to the absence of DLP MP Douglass Sherman, the deciding vote, whose carriage to Montiablo was delayed by inclement weather. This event would accelerate calls by the left to establish a national rail system, as well as a proper re-organization of Caprica’s roads. This government did not last long, as Scarlett faced two successive votes of no confidence, resigning right before the passage of the second. Her successor, Lex Anne-Frank, is widely remembered as the country’s railroad-king, leading the creation of several rail lines to connect the country following the Douglass fiasco.
Qumrani Oil Crisis
        The Caprican market-liberal right enjoyed a third electoral success with the nomination of Prime Minister Brigitte Nyborg in 1905, widely considered the worst Prime Minister in Caprican history. Unconcerned with politics, the Nyborg administration led to the formation of a strong left-wing, and was by-and-large carried by the efforts of Deputy Prime Minister Drew’s Liberal Party.
In 1907, the First World War broke out across Albueman. As part of efforts by allies of  Alania to cripple Urlavosk’s economy, the Strait Cooperation Council blockaded the straits of Marisol, cutting the world off from Qumrani oil. Having defeated the Qumrani navy 20 years prior, the SCC’s blockade was largely uncontested, and international protests drew stark condemnation to the Alanian side.
Then-Minister of State Gunther Fehlinger, head of the Caprican Foreign Ministry, led a diplomatic response to the oil crisis, establishing a precedent of Caprican internationalism. Along with his legislation which established Caprica’s maritime borders and organized the Foreign Ministry, Gunther is the first of many politicians to break from a century of Caprican isolationist policy.
Left-wing Governments
In 1909, George Brown, leader of the opposition, became Prime Minister. His tenure was marked by a major re-alignment to left-wing policies, and the passage of a flurry of legislation. These policies, aimed at establishing a larger social safety net for Capricans, were strongly opposed by the right-wing opposition at the time. Nevertheless, the George administration enjoyed a comfortable majority, and led the domestic response to the Qumrani oil crisis, establishing Caprican energy independence through long term drilling off the Chasmian sea
Foreign policy remained identical under the leadership of Violet Marin, who worked with former Foreign Minister Gunther Fehlinger to continue internationalist policies regardless of government. This interaction is alleged to be the start of the Caprican deep-state.
AFUERA
In 1913, the right returned to power, with Drew Adriasola becoming Prime Minister. The right, led by the Free Liberal Party, passed the controversial AFUERA Act, which repealed all left-wing legislation from the previous term. This led to widespread outrage, but the effects on the economy were not immediately felt by Caprican citizens. Two years into the term, former Prime Minister Alan would re-assume power as part of a deal with the Liberal Party.
The left returned with a vengeance with the election of Prime Minister Jacob Dyke in 1917. Yet the proposed budget by the Prime Minister was so terrible, the stock market collapsed overnight. Even today, public debate lingers as to whether the economic collapse was preceded by AFUERA, or entirely as a result of Jacob’s budget. Nevertheless, Jacob faced the fastest vote of no confidence in Caprican history, and was replaced by centrist Prime Minister Kosta.
Interwar Period
In 1922, the left won a second consecutive victory with the accession of Newjersey Roundhead to the premiership. Much like the George administration, the Newjersey Roundhead government proceeded with a flurry of legislation, this time with the guarantee that no AFUERA act would be repeated to undo a term’s worth of process. Much of Caprica’s social and infrastructure legislation was passed during the first Roundhead term.
The Roundhead government was succeeded by the 1926 right-wing victory, during which Jasmin Barnes, younger sister of former-PM Scarlett and a member of the Alliance party, became the Prime Minister. This election marked the return of Kaden Powell to politics, leading the United Caprica coalition–by now a collection of conservatives, reactionaries, and the old nobility.
The return of Kaden to politics outlined a growing problem in the Caprican election-system: the exorbitant power of the nobility, particularly amongst the conservative bloc. Amongst the traditionally moderate Caprican left, this inflamed tensions, leading to a return to Rochelist sympathies and the ultimate formation of the Caprican Communist Party. Opposition to the influence of the nobility among conservatives was concentrated largely in the Progressive Conservative movement, which sought to detach Caprican conservatism from the influence of Kaden’s bloc of nobles.
In 1931, Newjersey Roundhead returned to the premiership, having formed the Popular Front–an electoral alliance between the Democrats and the Communist Party. With a closely contested result, the centrist Liberal Democrats of Caprica and Progressive Conservatives chose to align with the left, under the condition that the communist party would be excluded from government in a confidence and supply agreement. 
This landmark coalition marked the exclusion of the growing Rochelist party and the conservative nobility from government, during which the Liberal Democrats under Gunther Fehlinger led the creation of the Western Columbian Community. Ostensibly an economic and political bloc of states in Columbia, it was perceived as a counter to growing Maraiseillan influence, through a military alliance with Bellefleur, Arska, and later Kromia.
Food Regime
The second Roundhead government collapsed after serious clashes between the Liberal Democrats and the Communists, with the abdication of Roundhead leading to a period of lawlessness and anarchy on the streets of Montiablo. This led to a surprise march on Montiablo by ILikeFood, a populist liberal democrat who was careened to national headlines by deep dissatisfaction in what appeared to be a binary choice between the rochelists and nobility. Careened into the premiership, ILikeFood fended off his first vote of no confidence with ease, but was assassinated in Kazana by terrorists. 
Following the Food regime, a temporary administration by the widely respected former President Bush was established, in which legislation was passed to ensure a basic continuity of governance in the event of a similar crisis.

Second World War
By 1935, the conservatives won a majority in the elections, with an expectation that Cara Tracey, a long-time conservative leader, would become the Prime Minister. However, the conservatives were pressed by the nobility to endorse Kaden as Prime Minister, marking the beginning of Kaden Powell’s administration. With the rochelist movement evaporating during the elections, the New Democratic Party, now led by Violet Marin, chose to dissolve the Popular Front–joining the Liberal Democrats and Progressive Conservatives in the Renaissance bloc.
Dogged by disagreements and accusations of incompetence, the Kaden administration was largely ineffective in governance. But the biggest upset came following the withdrawal of Marseillan recognition of neighboring Bellefleur. Having allied with Uralvosk earlier in the year in the landmark Treaty of Saint-Léon, Maraiseille had aligned itself with anti-Alanian powers. The response of the Kaden government, orchestrated by Minister of Justice Gavin, was to expel the diplomats of Maraiseille–an act which outraged the axis bloc and dashed any efforts at a peaceful reconciliation.
In 1938, Maraiseille conducted a surprise-attack on Montiablo, in a massive bombing raid supplemented by naval landings in Moeras. This marked the beginning of the Columbian theatre of the Second World War, in which Caprican and Maraiseillan forces fought, primarily in Bellefleur, and across the Aortic Sea. The initial Maraiseillan offensive, catching the Western Columbian Community’s forces unprepared, pushed deep into Bellefleur. However, subsequent Caprican efforts by Lyn Kozak Olds to hunt and destroy the Maraiseillan navy led the frontlines to gradually become stalemated.
As part of efforts to cripple the Maraiseillan economy, Lyn authorized the mass-smuggling of fentanyl into the Columbian mainland.
In Albueman, the Second World War began with the Uralvoskian invasion of Gallia, prompting Leisland and Alania to declare war on the Uralvoskian regime. Leisland fell relatively shortly after, following which Neustrasia aligned itself with the Axis bloc. As Uralvosk’s forces won victory after victory in Albueman, Alanian forces maintained firm control over their colonies, quelling various independence movements loosely or directly aligned with the Axis powers. After a failed attempt to soften the Alanian air force ahead of an expected naval invasion of the Alanian islands, Uralvosk abandoned efforts to conquer Alania, focusign instead on the interdiction of Alanian trade using submarines. This led to a direct hit on a Caprican freighter, killing hundreds, and leading to widespread condemnation of Uralvosk’s policy of unrestricted submarine warfare. In response to Alania’s quelling of independence movements in its colonies, the Tsang Union of Syndicates, a rochelist nation, which led to the Tsang entry into the war. Particularly in Asiana, where rochelist movements had long protested Alanian colonialism in the region, the joining of the Axis by the Tsang Union was seen as a betrayal, leading to many rochelist nations denouncing the Tsang cause and joining the allies. In particular, the entry of Da Nang and Maslyakhat into the war led to a massive number of deaths in Asiana, where high population density and imbalances in technological advances led to heavily one-sided slaughters by elite Albueman-trained forces encountering conscripted soldiers.
Uralvosk’s leadership, under the control of Aleksandr Krupitzyn, engaged in policies of genocide. Seeking an expansion of the Uralvoskian state, he released a book, Imperiya, where he classified various nations as Uralic, lesser Uralic, non-Uralic, or subhuman. In his work, Capricans were initially classified as lesser Uralic, but subsequent classifications in response to the alliance with Maraiseille and the Caprican entry into the war led to Caprica’s downgrade to subhuman.[2]

Fehlinger Administration
In 1939, the Liberal Democrats, Progressive Conservatives, and New Democratic Party won a contentious election against the United Caprica coalition. In the midst of this election, Kaden Powell was assassinated by Umeet JR., who some nobles have alleged (without evidence) was a COIN operative. The election was won narrowly, with two recounts occurring in the Montiablo elections, in which Fehlinger narrowly won, securing the votes to form government with a wide coalition of centrists, the left-wing, and notable right-wing defectors such as Aalborg Lindegaard and Jasmin Barnes, whose defection symbolized a fracturing of the nobility bloc.
The death of Kaden Powell was particularly notable in that the influence of the nobility on the conservative movement in Caprica collapsed. Seeing no further obstacles to unification, the Progressive Conservatives and Alliance Party united to form the Conservative Union, under the leadership of Cara Tracey, who famously dissolved the United Caprica electoral alliance. Up until the election of Fehlinger, Tracey had been chiefly responsible for organizing the response against Maraiseille, often hailed as the ‘Acting Prime Minister’ during the latter days of the Kaden administration.
The Fehlinger administration was known for its significant contribution to the allied cause during the world war, extensive use of COIN in Asiana (along with the assassination of Tricky Lindegaard, a known fascist and Axis-sympathizer), and the secret development of Caprican nuclear weapons. With a broad government coalition, the Fehlinger administration set about creating a robust bureaucracy, including the creation of nonpartisan advisory councils. Fehlinger died due to complications during open heart surgery, and was succeeded by Violet Marin of the New Democratic Party. Violet presided over the landings in Leisland and the end of the Second World War, including the atomic bombings of two Maraiseillan cities.
Post-war Reconstruction
        In 1945, Cara Tracey was nominated to the premiership, leading efforts to reconstruct the Caprican economy to reflect a stronger, more robust role in the global trade system. In particular, Cara dealt with the lingering drug crisis in Maraiseille, which had begun to spread into Caprica. Despite fair success, she faced serious obstruction from the Scarfelts Party, a vestige of noble-influence formerly in the United Caprica bloc, which unsuccessfully attempted to oust her in a vote of no confidence. While short lived, the attempted ouster undermined faith in Caprican elections, with the situation spiraling out of control following uncertainties over the control of the Caprican military.
Caprican Rochelist Republic
1951 Coup
In the summer of 1951, a coup was executed by members of the Republic Guard, aided and abetted by disgraced general Lyn Kozak Olds. The coup led to the dissolution of Parliament, and the establishment of a socialist dictatorship sympathetic to the OURS. This brazen act led to a long period of decline and isolationism within Caprica, considered a rogue state by neighboring countries. As a result, Caprica remained largely insulated from the economic boom of the 1970s,  in which Alanian decline competed with a worldwide strategy to contain the communist OURS movement.
Release of the Fehlinger Papers
Although initially facing stiff opposition, the declassification of the Fehlinger papers, which revealed the existence of a long-established Caprican deep-state which had dismantled communism and Kadenism, led to many rochelists and paternalists rallying around the Diana regime. This unlikely alliance, consisting of either extreme of Caprican politics, seized power from the center. With the exile of the Bush family, which resettled along with many Caprican exiles in Luminaire, all vestiges of the former republic were gone.
Rochelist Reforms
As part of efforts to completely revamp the state, the Diana regime eliminated all previous legislation, including an annulling of the old Constitution. The Diana regime has been called totalitarian by some, while others have defended the regime for reasserting Caprican sovereignty and promoting social welfare. Alignment with the OURS saw an upending of traditional Caprican relations, with an emphasis on third world outreach to Enyktia and Asiana. This greatly enhanced Caprican relations with the global south, but hindered Caprica’s development and access to more developed economies, primarily during the IT explosion in Trinconpore and Alania.
International Developments during the Regime
In Columbia, the vacuum filled by the absence of Caprica led to Trinconpore, traditionally a neutral nation, joining and establishing control over the Western Columbian Community. Steering the organization into a more pluralistic community, Trinconpore’s leadership saw the admission of Maraiseille and a policy of rapprochement between East and West. With respect to Maraiseille, the Western Columbian Community remained firm: no liberalization, no cooperation.  
Over time, Maraiseille became a secondary powerbroker within the organization. By 1970, the Western Columbian Community had been renamed as the Columbian Union. While allied to Alanian interests, the Columbian Union represented a distinct international bloc within the Western powers, concerned primarily with economic development.
As the OURS began to undergo reforms, Diana renounced Orensean revisionism, claiming the abandonment of the ‘continuous revolution’ and the pacification of relations with Alania was tantamount to a betrayal of the proletariat. Her totalitarian model, known as Allarism, drew increasingly sharp criticism from the rest of the world. 1971 is considered the year OURS ‘gave up’ on Caprica as a rochelist partner–abstaining on a critical UN security council vote that saw Alania’s naval base on Myrati, loaned during the World Wars, recognized internationally as Alanian territory.[3]
Collapse of the Regime
By 1982, the Diana regime had grown weak. Facing internal challenges after a three-decade reign, citizens were discontent with the economic conditions in Caprica, particularly following the computer-boom elsewhere. With hardliner Izan considered a next-in-line for power, exiled Capricans sought Alanian and Trinconporean aid. In 1983, the dam finally broke. Forces loyal to General Ar-an Park, joint chief of staff in the Caprican People’s Liberation Forces, in collaboration with the intelligence services of Alania and Trinconpore, staged a coup of the Caprican government. As Ar-an’a forces locked down Montiablo, racing to the Parliament in a mad dash against opposing forces loyal to the Diana regime, stealth helicopters from Trinconpore’s navy ferried hundreds of Alanian-trained Caprican exiles to airfields in southeastern Caprica, seizing control and allowing the landing of more exile forces using Trinconpore heavy air transports. In Myrati, Alanian forces seized control of Port Aaron, assuming temporary control of the island while the coup progressed.
Following the summary execution of Diana and Izan, for the first time in thirty-three years, Caprica was a free nation. As part of negotiations with the transitional government, the 1984 elections will feature repatriated Capricans from abroad.


Discussion
        Freedom did not come lightly. With over six-hundred protestors and soldiers dead, tensions remained high between the provisional government, supporters of liberalization, and citizens sympathetic to the regime. As citizens head to the ballot ahead of the 1984 general election, the first elections considered ‘fair and free’ since the end of the war, the world watches as Capricans, once more in command of their fate, chart their path at home and abroad.
Caprica stands between several paths, each representing an embrasure of several ‘legacies’ from the country’s previous leadership. While a majority of Caparica’s former ruling class are dead or retired, many of the country’s leaders have descendants, whether by blood or ideology, ready to take up the torch in the new government. Many other Capricans, such as the exiled Bush family, have taken the opportunity to return to their now-liberated country. The balance of power between Caprican exiles and Capricans who lived through the regime, even those on the same political axis, will be hard-felt.
With elections due on March 18th, 1984, Capricans prepare for the alignment of their nation to change dramatically, for the better or worse. For the first time in decades, the conservative parties of Caprica, including the Conservative Union, which maintained a government-in-exile on Trinconpore for the majority of the regime’s duration. Other right-wing parties, such as the Patriots for Caprica party, denounce a return to what was considered overtly liberal, internationalist policies. Most left-wing parties, including the New Democratic Party and splinter Social Democratic Party, have denounced the former regime. Nevertheless, various smaller parties exist to fill the various niches in Caprica’s political tapestry.
